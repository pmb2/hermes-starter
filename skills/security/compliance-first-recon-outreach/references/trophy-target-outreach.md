# Trophy Target Outreach Playbook

## When to Use This

A "trophy target" is a business that:
- Already knows they have a security problem (breached before, fined, under consent order)
- Has budget and compliance pressure
- Requires a different approach than the walk-in small business pitch
- Typically 50+ employees, professional services, with established IT/compliance functions

## The Approach

### Step 1: Identify Decision-Makers

**Who to target (in order):**
1. **Managing Partner / CEO** — strategic direction, approves spend
2. **Partner / Director in charge of compliance or risk** — day-to-day owner of security
3. **General Counsel** — if they've been fined, legal is involved

**Email format discovery:**
- Check their website's /experts or /team page for listed emails
- Search public documents (RFP responses, state contracts, annual reports) for email patterns
- Common format patterns: `firstinitiallastname@domain.com`, `first.last@domain.com`, `firstl@domain.com`
- Confirm format via cross-referencing multiple found addresses

### Step 2: Craft the Outreach Email

**Key rules for trophy targets:**
- Reference their known incident matter-of-factly, not as a threat
- Lead with respect for what they've been through, not fear
- Explain who you are and why you're contacting them specifically
- Offer a low-friction next step (20-minute meeting, not a full proposal)
- Don't lead with "I found vulnerabilities on your website" — they're already sensitive

**Subject line:** "[Service type] — [City]"

**Structure:**
1. Opening: Who you are, why you're writing
2. Relevance: Reference their known situation (briefly, respectfully)
3. Offer: What you do, how it's different
4. Next step: Low-friction meeting invitation
5. Close: Follow-up commitment

### Step 3: Send to Multiple Recipients

Send to 2-3 people simultaneously:
- The top decision-maker (Managing Partner)
- A more accessible partner/director who's publicly listed
- This ensures the email reaches someone even if the top person delegates

### Step 4: Follow-Up Cadence

| Day | Action |
|-----|--------|
| Tuesday AM | Send initial email |
| Thursday PM | Follow-up call — ask for either recipient |
| Day 7 | Brief follow-up email referencing a relevant local finding |
| Day 14 | If no response, either move on or walk in person (if geographically close) |

**Follow-up call script:**
> "Hi, this is [Name] for [recipient]. I sent them an email earlier this week about [service]. I'm following up to see if there's any interest."

### Step 5: In-Person Visit (Optional, Day 14+)

If they're nearby and haven't responded, walk in:
> "I sent an email to [names] a couple weeks ago about security assessments. I was in the area and figured I'd stop by. Is either of them available for 5 minutes?"

## What NOT to Do

| Don't | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Lead with "I found vulnerabilities on your website" | They're already sensitive about security post-breach |
| Price too low | Trophy targets have budgets — $7,500 minimum. Don't signal cheap. |
| Email from personal/Gmail address | Use a professional domain or they'll assume spam |
| Mention competitors by name | You're not competing with CrowdStrike or Kroll — you're offering a different tier of service |
| Threaten or imply consequences | "Your next breach will be worse" sounds predatory, not consultative |

## Email Template

```
Subject: Security assessment — [Company Name], [City]

[Names],

I'm a security consultant based in [City]. I came across your firm when reading about the [regulatory body] settlement — I know you've been dealing with the aftermath and the Corrective Action Plan.

I work with [industry] firms on external security assessments — finding what's publicly visible before someone else finds it. I focus on [geographic area] because the threat landscape here is active and local firms need access to this work without the big-firm price tag.

I'm not selling a product or a retainer. I do a one-time surface scan that maps your external digital footprint and identifies exposures. It's passive, nondisruptive, and takes about a week.

If you're open to it, I'd be happy to come by your office for 20 minutes and show you a sample of what a scan looks like for a firm like yours. If it's useful, great. If not, no harm done.

I'll follow up with a call later this week.

Best,
[Name]
[Phone]
```

## Worked Example: Beacon Stone CPAs LLP

**Background:** Latham, NY accounting firm. Ransomware in 2019. $175K HHS OCR settlement in Aug 2025. Under 2-year Corrective Action Plan. 136 employees. 50-year-old firm.

**Decision-makers found:**
- Ronald "Ron" Guzior — Managing Partner — rguzior@beacon-stone.example
- Brendan K. Kennedy — Partner — bkennedy@beacon-stone.example

**Email format:** `firstinitiallastname@beacon-stone.example` (confirmed by multiple public records)

**Email sent to both simultaneously.**

**Pre-qualification note:** They also offer vCISO/GRC services to their clients. This means they UNDERSTAND the value proposition — they just need it applied to themselves. The pitch isn't "what is security" — it's "let us help you with what you sell to others."
